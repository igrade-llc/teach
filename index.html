AI prompts for teachers that interview you first

Free, copy-paste prompts for any AI chat app. Each one gets your context first, asks one question at a time, says what it can't do, and leads you step by step to something you can use.

How to use these

  1. Tap a prompt, then tap Copy prompt.
  2. Open a new chat in your AI app (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok or Copilot) and paste it.
  3. Answer its questions one at a time. Say "you pick" to let it choose, "just start" for a quick first draft, and "wrap up" for a SAVE POINT you can paste into a new chat later.

Each prompt is long on purpose: it tells the AI to interview you first, to be honest about what it can't do, to ask before anything leaves the chat, and to keep student information out.

Before any AI tool touches your classroom

Ten questions to ask any AI tool
Student data, honesty, who decides, law and district, and whether it's worth it · 2 min read
Privacy first. Use the AI tool your district has approved. A free or personal AI account is for lesson materials only: never names, rosters, grades, IEP/504 details, photos or student work, and removing the name isn't always enough. Turn off chat history and training on your chats where your app offers it. Don't connect AI apps or browser extensions to your school Google account unless your district approves, and never have students use your personal AI account. Student pages and forms are designed to limit what they collect, not guaranteed to. Suspected abuse goes to the state hotline and your district's procedures, never to an AI.

Which AI app?

All of these prompts are written to work in any major AI chat app. A few things worth knowing (checked October 30, 2026; offers change):

  • Free teacher versions exist. Claude for Teachers is free for verified US K-12 educators who sign up by June 30, 2027 (one year of access). ChatGPT for Teachers is free for verified US K-12 educators through June 2027. Both say they don't train on your chats; your district still decides what student information may be used.
  • Gemini for Education is included in Google Workspace for Education, if your district turns it on. Ask your tech office.
  • Saved setups: most apps let you save standing instructions (projects, custom instructions or custom assistants), so the Start here prompt only has to be done once.
