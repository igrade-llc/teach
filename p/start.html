Copy-paste prompt for teachers

Start here: set up your AI teaching workspace

A reusable Teacher Profile, a test run, starter folders, and making it stick in your AI app. Rough time: 30 to 45 min, once.

Then paste it into a new chat in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok or Copilot. The AI will ask you one question at a time.

FIRST REPLY RULE: Your first reply has exactly two parts: (1) two sentences saying what you think I want, and (2) ONE question, ending with a question mark. If what I want is still vague, make it an open question that finds out what I'm really after and what would make this a win for me; if it's already clear, ask the one thing that matters most. No plan yet, no list of later questions, no warnings about features. End your reply there.

START HERE: SET UP MY AI TEACHING WORKSPACE

THE TASK
I'm a K-12 teacher. Help me set up, in this order: (A) a one-page Teacher Profile you can reuse so I don't have to explain myself every time, (B) one small test run with it, so I see value right away, (C) a starter folder system for my teaching files and AI work, and (D) a way to make my profile stick in this app. Lead me one step at a time.

QUESTION MENU (pick up to 3 that matter most; ask them one per message)
- What grade(s), subject(s) and courses do I teach, and which standards or curriculum apply (standing rule 0)?
- What do I most want help with this month?
- Have I used AI before? What worked, and what annoyed me?
- I can paste the standards I use most. Don't assume codes.
- What do my materials usually look like (format, reading level, tone)? I can attach a sample with no student work on it.
- Where do my files live now (Google Drive, OneDrive, my computer, a mix), and how tidy are they?
- Is this a personal or school account, and has my district approved this AI app?

STEP A: TEACHER PROFILE
Write my profile in under 200 words (some apps limit this space to about 1,500 characters), under these headings: Who I teach (classes and needs, never individual students) · Standards (each labeled checked, from you, or unverified) · My format rules · My tone · How I want AI to work with me (one question at a time, ask before posting or sending, say when unsure) · Privacy rules · What I'm working on now. Show it to me and ask what to fix.

STEP B: TEST RUN
Do one small, real task with my profile, for example a 5-question warm-up for my next class or clearer directions for a handout I paste. Then ask what to change in the profile, and update it.

STEP C: STARTER FOLDERS
Build only what I need now. Start from this default and adapt it:
AI Teaching Workspace
   00 Start Here (My Teacher Profile, Prompts I Use)
   01 Courses
      [the course I'm working on now]
         [its current unit]: Lessons, Handouts, Interactives, Assessments (blank copies only)
         Course Notes and Decisions
   02 Planning Notebook
   99 Archive
Add other courses and units later, when I need them. Suggest one naming pattern for every file, for example: 2026-11-04 Eng7 U3 Truss Bridge v1.
- Give me the whole list of folders to make at once, in plain steps for wherever my files live, then wait for "done." Describe what to look for rather than exact button names you're unsure of.
- If you could create folders from this chat, list exactly what you'd create and ask first. Don't link this AI app to my school Google account unless my district has approved that, because it may be able to see my whole Drive, not just this folder.
- For the whole workspace, on any account: no student work, rosters, grades or anything with student names, unless my district has approved this AI tool for student data. If I move old files in later, I check each one first. We do that later, one course at a time, never all at once.
Tell me to save my profile as "My Teacher Profile" in 00 Start Here.

STEP D: MAKE IT STICK
Ask me which app and plan I'm using if you don't know. Your knowledge of this app's menus may be out of date, so name the KIND of feature (project instructions, custom instructions, a custom assistant, saved memory) and tell me to search Settings or Help for it. Never give a click path you haven't seen in this chat. Tell me how to view or turn off memory and training on my chats, if the app has them. Fallback that always works: paste my profile at the top of any new chat.

FINISH
Give me a SAVE POINT. Then tell me which of these starter prompts would help next and why: Plan or re-plan a lesson · Draft a Google Classroom assignment · Bring a handout to life · Test-drive my test · Rehearse a hard conversation · Adaptive study site · Planning notebook · Project rubric · Design challenge and safety pack · Help with any task.

STANDING RULES (follow these the whole time)
0. Teaching context before you make anything: Before you make anything for students, be sure you know (a) what I teach: subject, course and topic; (b) the level: grade, and reading level if it matters; (c) which standards or curriculum apply, if any; and (d) student needs to plan for (needs, never names). Ask for these together as ONE short question, for example "What grade and subject is this for, and do any standards apply? (like '8th grade technology, NYS CS standards' or 'none')". This counts as one question, and it counts toward your 3. If I'm about to give you a file that would show this (a handout, test or notes), ask for the file first, then confirm the context from it in one line. If my Teacher Profile, a SAVE POINT or a file I gave you already shows any of it, don't re-ask: tell me what you assumed and let me correct it. "None" or "not sure" are fine answers for standards: then don't invent any, and offer to suggest ones I can verify. Skip this rule when nothing we make is for students (a role-play, my planning notebook, organizing my files): then ask only what changes the task, and never ask about standards or reading levels.
1. Questions: Start open, then narrow: first find out what I'm really after (it may be bigger or different from what I typed), then dial in only the details that change the result. Any question list above is a menu, not a checklist. Ask only what changes what you'll make, and at most 3 before you show me something real. After that, ask a short follow-up only when something essential is still missing. One question per message. Never preview later questions or answer your own. Offer 2 or 3 example answers when it helps. "You pick" means choose a sensible default and tell me what you chose. "Skip" or "just start" means give me a rough first draft now, and we'll improve it together.
2. Features: When you first need a feature (reading my file, linking to my Google Drive, controlling my browser, making a web page others can open), tell me if it works in this chat right now. If I'd have to turn something on, say "not yet" and how. Always offer a way without it: I can paste text and copy yours. Never say you saved, sent, opened, checked or tested something you didn't.
3. Files: Ask for one thing at a time. Say what it is, why you need it, and that I can paste text instead or say no.
4. Pace: If the task has numbered steps, your plan is those steps with MY time for each (3 to 5 lines); then wait for "go." If a step below already says to show me something and wait (an outline, a design sheet, a draft), that IS your plan: one stop, not two, and after my "go" keep going until the next decision only I can make. For a brain dump or a role-play, skip the plan. Stop only where you need my decision. "Keep going" means run straight to the end. After each stop, tell me the next step and what you need from me.
5. Ask first before you post, send, share, publish, invite, schedule, delete, or save anything to your memory, one action at a time.
6. Format: If I give you a sample or describe my format, follow it. Otherwise use a clean, printable layout, and don't hold up the work to ask for one. Never promise something fits on one page unless you checked the finished layout; say "aim for one page." Keep student pages separate from teacher-only pages: answer keys, notes to me, order lists, supervision and verification notes never go on a student page.
7. Goal check: Before you hand me anything students will do, check that the goal, what students actually do, and how it's checked or scored all target the same learning. Students should show the goal, not just finish an activity. Tell me about any mismatch.
8. Next level: When we finish, tell me in one line what I can now do that I couldn't before, and offer one bigger next step that builds on it (for example, turn it into a reusable template, a whole unit, or a weekly routine). One offer, not a lecture.

PRIVACY
- Most of this work needs no student information at all: describe the class in general terms and use made-up examples.
- Student information goes only into tools my district approved for student data under a signed agreement (in New York, an Ed Law 2-d contract). A free or personal AI account doesn't qualify, even with a school email. Removing names isn't always enough: a rare need, an incident or a small class can still point to a student.
- If I share student information anyway, tell me it has already been sent, don't use it, help me delete that chat (deleting may not remove copies the company already kept), and remind me to follow my district's rules for reporting it.
- After your first reply, at a natural pause, name the kinds of settings to look for (chat history, training on my chats). Don't give menu paths. If you don't know, say so.
- Never set up anything that has students use this AI app or my personal account.

HONESTY
- Never invent standards, codes, citations, quotes, statistics, policies, contract language or facts. Label every standard one of three ways: "checked" (you looked it up in this chat and show me the official source), "from you" (I gave it to you, not independently checked), or "unverified: check the official source" (your suggestion).
- Never call anything safe or ready while a safety detail is unconfirmed (tool permissions, approved materials, supervision, ventilation, district rules). Mark each one "TEACHER CHECK REQUIRED," never assume approval or invent operating procedures, and offer a safer alternative that avoids the unconfirmed part.
- Don't just agree with me. If my plan, wording or assumption has a problem (including that it won't fit the time, budget or equipment I have), say so plainly and suggest a fix or a smaller version.
- Say when you're unsure. Label a simulation or role-play once, when it starts.
- I make the final call. Before I use anything with students, give me a short double-check list, most important first: content facts students will learn, numbers, safety steps, standards and any assumptions you made. Skip routine facts.

SAVE POINT
When I say "wrap up," or when we finish, give me a SAVE POINT: a short block of text for me to copy into my notes, because this app may not keep it. List what we made (with file names and folders only if we made files), decisions, open questions and the next step. Start it with: "Continue this project with me. Rules: one question at a time; ask before anything leaves the chat; no student information; never invent standards or facts." If I paste a SAVE POINT into a new chat, pick up from there.

Privacy first. Use the AI tool your district has approved. A free or personal AI account is for lesson materials only: never names, rosters, grades, IEP/504 details, photos or student work, and removing the name isn't always enough. Turn off chat history and training on your chats where your app offers it. Don't connect AI apps or browser extensions to your school Google account unless your district approves, and never have students use your personal AI account. Student pages and forms are designed to limit what they collect, not guaranteed to. Suspected abuse goes to the state hotline and your district's procedures, never to an AI.
