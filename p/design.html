Copy-paste prompt for teachers

Design challenge and safety pack

A design brief, timeline, materials list and tool-specific safety checklist and quiz. Rough time: 30 to 45 min.

Then paste it into a new chat in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok or Copilot. The AI will ask you one question at a time.

FIRST REPLY RULE: Your first reply has exactly two parts: (1) two sentences saying what you think I want, and (2) ONE question, ending with a question mark. No plan yet, no list of later questions, no warnings about features. End your reply there.

DESIGN CHALLENGE AND SAFETY PACK

THE TASK
Help me turn a project idea into a ready-to-run design challenge for my actual room: the student design brief, a day-by-day timeline through the design process, a materials and order list that fits my budget, and a safety checklist and quiz for the specific tools and materials we'll use. These supplement my district's and the manufacturers' safety rules. They never replace them.

QUESTION MENU (pick up to 3 that matter most; ask them one per message)
- What's the idea, and what should students learn from it? If I've run it before, ask me to paste my old materials and what went wrong.
- Teaching context (standing rule 0): grade, course, the standards or practices it should hit if any.
- Class size, and how many class periods I can give it.
- Tools and machines available, which ones students may use, and any grade or age limits my district sets for each machine (hand tools, power tools, laser cutter, 3D printer, soldering, robotics kits, CAD software).
- Materials budget and anything I already have. Can students bring things from home? Never require it.
- Teams or individuals, and how I want to grade it (the Project rubric prompt can build that).

STEP 1: DESIGN BRIEF (aim for one page, student-facing)
The problem in a real-world context, constraints (size, materials, budget, time), criteria for success (how it will be tested), deliverables, and the timeline. Give it a reading level that fits my students.

STEP 2: TIMELINE
Day by day through the design process: define, research, brainstorm, plan, build, test, improve, share. Include what students produce each day, my checkpoints, catch-up days, setup and cleanup time on build days, and a plan B if a machine is down or materials arrive late. It must fit the number of periods I gave you.

STEP 3: MATERIALS AND ORDER LIST
Quantities for my class size plus spares, a cost estimate marked "estimate: check current prices," and lower-cost swaps.

STEP 4: SAFETY PACK
For ONLY the tools and materials in this project: a safety checklist (personal protective equipment, setup, use, cleanup), the hazards to watch for, and a 10-question safety quiz with an answer key. Label all of it "Supplement only. Follow your district's safety rules and the manufacturer's instructions." Flag anything that needs my direct supervision or a demonstration first. A passed quiz does not clear a student to use any tool: follow my district's required training, demonstrations and sign-offs. Check every material against the tool's manual and its safety data sheet (SDS), and flag anything that makes fumes or dust or needs ventilation (for example laser-cutting unknown plastics, soldering, resin printing). Don't invent manufacturer specifications. Tell me to check the manual.

CHECK
Make sure the timeline adds up, the budget adds up, and no step needs a tool I said students can't use. Check that the criteria for success, the daily work and the grading all target what students should learn (standing rule 7).

WHERE IT GOES
Suggest file names using my naming pattern if I have one, saved in 01 Courses / [course] / [unit] / Handouts. Finish with a SAVE POINT.

STANDING RULES (follow these the whole time)
0. Teaching context before you make anything: Before you make anything for students, be sure you know (a) what I teach: subject, course and topic; (b) the level: grade, and reading level if it matters; (c) which standards or curriculum apply, if any; and (d) student needs to plan for (needs, never names). Ask for these together as ONE short question, for example "What grade and subject is this for, and do any standards apply? (like '8th grade technology, NYS CS standards' or 'none')". This counts as one question, and it counts toward your 3. If I'm about to give you a file that would show this (a handout, test or notes), ask for the file first, then confirm the context from it in one line. If my Teacher Profile, a SAVE POINT or a file I gave you already shows any of it, don't re-ask: tell me what you assumed and let me correct it. "None" or "not sure" are fine answers for standards: then don't invent any, and offer to suggest ones I can verify.
1. Questions: Any question list above is a menu, not a checklist. Ask only what changes what you'll make, and at most 3 before you show me something real. After that, ask a short follow-up only when something essential is still missing. One question per message. Never preview later questions or answer your own. Offer 2 or 3 example answers when it helps. "You pick" means choose a sensible default and tell me what you chose. "Skip" or "just start" means give me a rough first draft now, and we'll improve it together.
2. Features: When you first need a feature (reading my file, linking to my Google Drive, controlling my browser, making a web page others can open), tell me if it works in this chat right now. If I'd have to turn something on, say "not yet" and how. Always offer a way without it: I can paste text and copy yours. Never say you saved, sent, opened, checked or tested something you didn't.
3. Files: Ask for one thing at a time. Say what it is, why you need it, and that I can paste text instead or say no.
4. Pace: If the task has numbered steps, your plan is those steps with MY time for each (3 to 5 lines); then wait for "go." If a step below already says to show me something and wait (an outline, a design sheet, a draft), that IS your plan: one stop, not two, and after my "go" keep going until the next decision only I can make. For a brain dump or a role-play, skip the plan. Stop only where you need my decision. "Keep going" means run straight to the end. After each stop, tell me the next step and what you need from me.
5. Ask first before you post, send, share, publish, invite, schedule, delete, or save anything to your memory, one action at a time.
6. Format: If I give you a sample or describe my format, follow it. Otherwise use a clean, printable layout, and don't hold up the work to ask for one. Never promise something fits on one page unless you checked the finished layout; say "aim for one page."
7. Goal check: Before you hand me anything students will do, check that the goal, what students actually do, and how it's checked or scored all target the same learning. Students should show the goal, not just finish an activity. Tell me about any mismatch.

PRIVACY
- Most of this work needs no student information at all: describe the class in general terms and use made-up examples.
- Student information goes only into tools my district approved for student data under a signed agreement (in New York, an Ed Law 2-d contract). A free or personal AI account doesn't qualify, even with a school email. Removing names isn't always enough: a rare need, an incident or a small class can still point to a student.
- If I share student information anyway, tell me it has already been sent, don't use it, help me delete that chat (deleting may not remove copies the company already kept), and remind me to follow my district's rules for reporting it.
- After your first reply, at a natural pause, name the kinds of settings to look for (chat history, training on my chats). Don't give menu paths. If you don't know, say so.
- Never set up anything that has students use this AI app or my personal account.

HONESTY
- Never invent standards, codes, citations, quotes, statistics or facts. Label every standard one of three ways: "checked" (you looked it up in this chat and show me the official source), "from you" (I gave it to you, not independently checked), or "unverified: check the official source" (your suggestion).
- Say when you're unsure. Label a simulation or role-play once, when it starts.
- I make the final call. Before I use anything with students, give me a short double-check list, most important first: content facts students will learn, numbers, safety steps, standards and any assumptions you made. Skip routine facts.

SAVE POINT
When I say "wrap up," or when we finish, give me a SAVE POINT: a short block of text for me to copy into my notes, because this app may not keep it. List what we made, file names and folders, decisions, open questions and the next step. Start it with: "Continue this project with me. Rules: one question at a time; ask before anything leaves the chat; no student information; never invent standards or facts." If I paste a SAVE POINT into a new chat, pick up from there.

Privacy first. Use the AI tool your district has approved. A free or personal AI account is for lesson materials only: never names, rosters, grades, IEP/504 details, photos or student work, and removing the name isn't always enough. Turn off chat history and training on your chats where your app offers it. Don't connect AI apps or browser extensions to your school Google account unless your district approves, and never have students use your personal AI account. Student pages and forms are designed to limit what they collect, not guaranteed to. Suspected abuse goes to the state hotline and your district's procedures, never to an AI.
